# Daily Progressor

Create an automatic auto-generated news publication where every article is generated autonomously by lovable AI. 

The AI has a consistent mission: become the best progressive daily news explainer. The learning happens in how it covers the news and which stories deserve deeper attention, rather than changing its political orientation.



PRD: NewSlop (Working Title)



Vision



Build an autonomous progressive news publication that monitors U.S. politics, explains the day's most important events, publishes daily recaps and long-form explainers, and continuously improves based on reader engagement without manually written articles, but instead produced by Lovable AI or other LLM apis.

The goal is not to break news first, but to become the publication that best helps readers understand what happened, why it matters, and what to watch next.

---

Mission



Every day, answer three questions:



What happened?



Why does it matter?



What should readers pay attention to next?





The publication acts as an AI editor, researcher, and explainer focused on progressive politics and policy.





---



Editorial Principles



The publication should strive to be:



Fact-based and well-sourced.



Transparent about where information comes from.



Clearly progressive in its editorial framing and policy interests.



Distinguish factual reporting from analysis and opinion.



Update articles when new information becomes available.



Correct errors openly.







---



Daily Workflow



Each morning and evening, the system:



1. Collects major U.S. political and cultural news.





2. Identifies the day's biggest developments.





3. Clusters similar stories.





4. Produces multiple types of content.





5. Publishes automatically.





6. Measures engagement.





7. Learns which formats and topics resonate.

Future Vision



The publication evolves into an AI-powered editorial desk rather than simply a news summarizer. It develops a library of explainers, timelines, and policy guides that become more valuable over time, while continuing to publish timely recaps each day.



One design choice I'd recommend is to separate article types visually. For example:



News – factual summaries of recent events.



Analysis – interpretation and implications, clearly labeled.



Explainers – evergreen background pieces.



Opinion – explicitly identified editorial viewpoints.





That distinction helps readers understand what they're reading and reinforces credibility. Even for a publication with a progressive perspective, clearly separating reporting, analysis, and opinion makes it easier for readers to trust the factual foundation while recognizing where interpretation begins.
